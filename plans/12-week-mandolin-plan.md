# 12-Week Daily Mandolin Learning Plan

Generated for Walker on 2026-05-09


Holding and stabilizing the mandolin

Core setup
- Use a strap even when seated. It should hold the instrument where your hands can relax, not merely prevent a fall.
- Bring the mandolin to your body; do not hunch your body down to the mandolin.
- Neck angle: slightly upward, roughly 20–35 degrees. If the neck is flat or diving, the left hand starts supporting weight instead of fretting.
- Body contact: stabilize with three light points—right forearm on/near the upper bout, back or side of mandolin against torso, lower bout/horn resting on thigh if seated.
- Left hand rule: the left hand should not be the instrument stand. You should be able to briefly release thumb pressure without the neck collapsing.

Seated options to test
1. Left-thigh classical-ish hold: mandolin rests more toward the left leg, neck angles up. Often best for clean fretting and small instruments.
2. Right-thigh casual hold: common but can let the neck drift down; use a strap and raise the neck if wrist bends sharply.
3. Standing-with-strap practice: 5 minutes daily early on. This prevents learning a seated-only posture that falls apart later.

Stabilization tools/options
- Strap: first-line fix. Leather, nylon, or mandolin-specific loop strap. Attach to endpin and scroll/headstock area depending on instrument design.
- Non-slip pad/cloth: useful if the mandolin slides on clothing. A small suede/chamois cloth between back and torso/thigh can help.
- Footstool or raised left foot: can help angle the instrument up, but avoid twisting your back.
- Chair choice: firm, armless chair; sit forward. Soft couches make the mandolin unstable.
- Professional setup: if you need excessive left-hand force, no holding trick will fix it. Check action/nut height/bridge.

Pain/tension warning signs
- Left wrist sharply bent, thumb squeezing, shoulder raised, forearm burning, fingertips needing extreme pressure, or neck diving. Fix posture/setup before adding speed.


Best-option gear/setup checklist

Must-have
- Clip-on tuner: any reliable chromatic tuner is enough at first.
- Picks: buy a variety pack around 0.73, 0.88, 1.0, 1.14, and 1.5 mm. Pick feel is personal; re-test in Week 9 for tremolo.
- Strap: mandatory if the instrument slides or the neck dives.
- Metronome: phone app is fine.

Playability checks
- If first-position notes feel painfully stiff or sound sharp, nut slots may be high.
- If fretted notes around 12th fret are out of tune, bridge intonation may need adjustment.
- If strings buzz, action may be too low or frets/setup need attention.
- Artist Guitars cites approximate action targets measured at 12th fret with first fret held: ~0.05 inch bass side and ~0.04 inch treble side; nut clearance around 0.012 inch G/D and 0.010 inch A/E. These are setup reference points, not a mandate.

Best beginner resource path by budget/style
- Free bluegrass/old-time: MandoLessons Beginner Series + David Benedict + Mandolin Compass chord pages.
- Paid structured bluegrass/old-time: Peghead Nation Beginning Mandolin.
- Irish/trad: OAIM Mandolin Basics; prioritize melody, listening, plectrum motion, ornamentation.
- Book support: Alfred's Teach Yourself to Play Mandolin or Hal Leonard Mandolin Method Book 1. Verify included audio/video access before buying; listings vary.

## Daily template
- Tune: 2–3 min
- Stabilization/posture check: 1 min
- Right hand: 5 min
- Scale/chords: 5–10 min
- Tune/song: 10–20 min
- Record/review: 1–2 min once or twice weekly


## Week 1: Stabilize, tune, and make clean sound

Goals:
- Comfortable seated posture
- Reliable G-D-A-E tuning
- Clean open-string downstrokes
- Know string names and basic tab

Best option this week: Best free path: MandoLessons Part 1 + David Benedict very-first lesson. Best purchase: clip-on tuner + several pick gauges. If mandolin feels painful, prioritize setup check before adding difficulty.

- **Day 1:** Instrument orientation: identify G/D/A/E courses; tune slowly; 5 min open-string downstrokes; test seated positions and strap height.
- **Day 2:** Posture drill: sit on front third of chair; mandolin angled slightly upward; 10 min open-string picking; watch for shoulder/wrist tension.
- **Day 3:** Learn tab basics; pick open strings G-D-A-E and back; metronome quarter notes at 60 bpm; record 30 sec.
- **Day 4:** Alternate picking intro: down-up on each course, 4 beats each; keep pick shallow; repeat tuning routine.
- **Day 5:** First melody by tab/ear, very slow; stop and fix buzzes; note whether action feels too stiff.
- **Day 6:** Review: open-string downstrokes, alternate picking, first melody phrase; 5-minute posture experiment standing with strap.
- **Day 7:** Light day: tune, play best melody phrase 5 times cleanly, watch one beginner video; write one setup/comfort issue to fix next week.

## Week 2: First chords and simple strumming

Goals:
- Two-finger G/C/D/F or easy G/C/D/A/Em
- Basic down-strum rhythm
- Chord changes without stopping

Best option this week: Best resource: Mandolin Compass two-finger chords for immediate success; MandoLessons Parts 2–3 for first chords/strum. Best accessory: strap if you do not already use one.

- **Day 8:** Learn two-finger G and D; 5 min open-string picking; 10 min G-D switching without tempo pressure.
- **Day 9:** Add C; practice G-C-D-G; strum once per beat at 60 bpm; keep left thumb relaxed.
- **Day 10:** Add A or Em; play D-G-A-D; isolate hardest chord change for 5 min.
- **Day 11:** Basic song: “She’ll Be Coming Round the Mountain” or “You Are My Sunshine” with two chords; slow and steady.
- **Day 12:** Strum pattern: down-down-up or bass-strum if comfortable; focus on steady beat more than volume.
- **Day 13:** Record one chord progression; watch for mandolin sliding/neck diving; adjust strap or contact points.
- **Day 14:** Review day: tune, play 3 progressions, one simple song; note which chords need cleaner fingers.

## Week 3: G major scale and first full tune

Goals:
- G major scale first position
- One complete beginner tune
- Cleaner left-hand fingertip placement

Best option this week: Best resource: MandoLessons Part 4–6. Best method: learn scale notes as tune vocabulary, not abstract exercise.

- **Day 15:** Learn G major scale slowly; use alternate picking only if clean; say string names.
- **Day 16:** G scale + first half of “Cindy” or “Boil Them Cabbage Down”; phrase-by-phrase.
- **Day 17:** Second half of tune; 5 min chord backup in G/C/D.
- **Day 18:** Metronome: play scale at 60 bpm quarter notes; tune phrase at half speed.
- **Day 19:** Memorization attempt: play first phrase without looking; check tab only after trying.
- **Day 20:** Record full tune slowly; circle one weak measure and loop it 10 times correctly.
- **Day 21:** Review: scale, chords, tune; play for enjoyment, no speed goals.

## Week 4: Timing, transitions, and first mini-performance

Goals:
- Steady rhythm
- One song + one tune start-to-finish
- No stopping after mistakes

Best option this week: Best option: backing tracks/play-alongs from MandoLessons or YouTube at 0.75 speed. Best habit: weekly phone video.

- **Day 22:** Warm-up open strings; G scale; play tune with metronome, ignoring small errors.
- **Day 23:** Chord-change day: G-C-D-G, 2 min each transition; then song backup.
- **Day 24:** Tune detail: fix hardest two measures only; do not restart from beginning repeatedly.
- **Day 25:** Play melody once, chords once; practice recovery after intentional missed note.
- **Day 26:** Slow play-along day; use YouTube speed control if needed.
- **Day 27:** Record mini-set: one chord song + one melody tune.
- **Day 28:** Rest/review: watch recording, write 3 observations: timing, tone, posture.

## Week 5: D major and fiddle-tune feel

Goals:
- D major scale
- D/G/A chord family
- Start “Angeline the Baker”

Best option this week: Best resource: Peghead Nation sample “Angeline the Baker” if you want structured phrase learning; MandoLessons/David Benedict free alternatives.

- **Day 29:** Learn D major scale; compare to G scale fingering; slow clean tone.
- **Day 30:** D-G-A chord switching; bass-strum pattern in D.
- **Day 31:** “Angeline the Baker” A-part, first phrase only; mark time with pick hand.
- **Day 32:** A-part second phrase; loop phrase endings; no speed.
- **Day 33:** Join A-part phrases; play D scale before tune.
- **Day 34:** Record A-part; check if pick hand stops during rests—keep time with “ghost” motion.
- **Day 35:** Review D scale/chords/A-part; optional listen to multiple recordings.

## Week 6: Right-hand discipline

Goals:
- Relaxed pick grip
- Even down-up strokes
- Crossing strings without panic

Best option this week: Best option: David Benedict right-hand video + Matt C Bruno picking-hand practice idea. If pain/tension appears, reduce speed and session length.

- **Day 36:** Open-string down-up, 4 beats per string; shake out hand every minute.
- **Day 37:** String crossing G-D, D-A, A-E; tiny pick motion; metronome 50–60 bpm.
- **Day 38:** Rest-stroke experiment: pick through course and land lightly on next; tone over speed.
- **Day 39:** Apply right-hand focus to “Angeline”; slow enough for even notes.
- **Day 40:** Chord backup + melody alternation; keep pick hand moving during rests.
- **Day 41:** Record close-up of picking hand; watch for locked wrist or deep pick digging.
- **Day 42:** Light review; choose one right-hand cue: loose, small, even, or shallow.

## Week 7: Rhythm backup and beginning chop concept

Goals:
- Stronger backup rhythm
- Bass-strum pattern
- Muted percussive chop concept

Best option this week: Best resource: Peghead Nation open-chords/rhythm section if subscribed. Free: Mandolin Compass chords + bluegrass rhythm demos. Do not force closed chop chords too early.

- **Day 43:** Review open chords G/C/D/A/Em; 2 min each clean fretting.
- **Day 44:** Bass-strum: low note then upper strings; use G-C-D progression.
- **Day 45:** Muted strum: release fretting pressure after strum; learn short sound.
- **Day 46:** Chop feel on beats 2 and 4 using easy muted chord; do not squeeze hard.
- **Day 47:** Backup for one tune: play chords while humming melody.
- **Day 48:** Record rhythm only with metronome; check if beats 2/4 are steady.
- **Day 49:** Review and fun play-through: two tunes, one song, one rhythm pattern.

## Week 8: Repertoire consolidation

Goals:
- 3–4 playable pieces
- One from memory
- Weekly recording habit

Best option this week: Best option: choose one primary course and stop resource-hopping. Free track: MandoLessons + David Benedict. Paid track: Peghead Nation for bluegrass/old-time; OAIM only if Irish is the goal.

- **Day 50:** List repertoire; choose 3 keepers and 1 stretch tune.
- **Day 51:** Keeper 1: melody + chords; isolate weak measure.
- **Day 52:** Keeper 2: melody + timing; play with metronome.
- **Day 53:** Keeper 3: chord backup; keep going through mistakes.
- **Day 54:** Stretch tune intro: first phrase only, very slow.
- **Day 55:** Record mini-set of 2 pieces; compare to Week 4.
- **Day 56:** Rest/review; clean instrument, change strings if dull or hard to tune.

## Week 9: Tremolo introduction

Goals:
- Smooth short tremolo bursts
- Loose wrist/forearm
- Use tremolo on long melody notes

Best option this week: Best option: learn tremolo as relaxed sustain, not speed. Mandozine and Peghead emphasize loose grip/metronome. Keep it short to avoid tension.

- **Day 57:** Open E tremolo: 2 seconds on, 4 seconds rest; 10 reps only.
- **Day 58:** Open A and E tremolo; listen for even volume; stop before fatigue.
- **Day 59:** Metronome tremolo subdivisions: slow down-up groups; no blur yet.
- **Day 60:** Add tremolo to one long note in a tune; keep rest of tune normal.
- **Day 61:** Compare pick gauges for tremolo comfort; note best feel/tone.
- **Day 62:** Record tremolo close-up; check for arm locking or death-grip.
- **Day 63:** Light review; tremolo 5 min max, then play easy repertoire.

## Week 10: A major and common jam keys

Goals:
- A major scale basics
- A/D/E chord family
- One A tune started

Best option this week: Best resource: Peghead first-five-tunes path or David Benedict/MandoLessons A tunes. Choose “Old Joe Clark” or “Cluck Old Hen.”

- **Day 64:** Learn A major scale slowly; note pinky stretch if used, do not strain.
- **Day 65:** A-D-E chord progression; compare with D/G/A feel.
- **Day 66:** Start “Old Joe Clark” or “Cluck Old Hen” phrase 1.
- **Day 67:** Phrase 2; listen repeatedly before playing.
- **Day 68:** Join phrases; play with metronome at slow tempo.
- **Day 69:** Record A tune fragment + A scale; identify intonation issues.
- **Day 70:** Review jam keys: G, D, A scales and I-IV-V chords.

## Week 11: Ear training and tab independence

Goals:
- Learn one phrase by ear
- Hum before playing
- Recognize home note/chord

Best option this week: Best option: use recordings before tab. OAIM explicitly recommends listening frequently before playing; this applies across styles.

- **Day 71:** Listen to chosen tune 5 times; hum first phrase; find first note.
- **Day 72:** Pick out first phrase by ear; check tab only after attempt.
- **Day 73:** Repeat with second phrase; slow, no shame, lots of listening.
- **Day 74:** Play phrase from memory; then play chords underneath if known.
- **Day 75:** Call-and-response: listen 5 sec, pause, play back.
- **Day 76:** Record ear-learned phrase; compare to source recording.
- **Day 77:** Review all repertoire without tab first; use tab only to repair.

## Week 12: Jam simulation and next-step decision

Goals:
- 3 tunes start-to-finish
- Rhythm + melody roles
- Choose next 3-month focus

Best option this week: Best next step: slow jam/play-along + one structured course. If you love melody: Irish/OAIM. If you love bluegrass backup/jams: Peghead/David Benedict. If budget-free: MandoLessons + Mandolin Compass + YouTube speed control.

- **Day 78:** Make jam list: 3 tunes, 2 chord songs, keys, starting notes.
- **Day 79:** Tune 1: melody once, chords once, no stopping.
- **Day 80:** Tune 2: melody once, chords once; recover from mistakes.
- **Day 81:** Tune 3 or song: play with backing track/YouTube slow speed.
- **Day 82:** Full mini-set rehearsal: count in, play, keep time, finish cleanly.
- **Day 83:** Final recording: 3-piece set; save as baseline.
- **Day 84:** Decision day: choose next path—bluegrass jam, Irish melody, classical tremolo, or song accompaniment; schedule next 12 weeks.
